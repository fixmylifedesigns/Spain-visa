Mui · Japan → Spain

Bringing Mui home to Spain

Everything Mui needs to fly from Japan and settle in with us in Valencia.

What the EU requires

  • An ISO microchip, implanted before the rabies vaccine.
  • A valid rabies vaccination. After a first-ever shot, wait at least 21 days before travel.
  • An EU animal health certificate from an official vet, issued no more than 10 days before arrival. Spain asks for it in Spanish as well as English.
  • A rabies antibody (titer) test is needed from most non-EU countries, but not from countries on the EU's list. Confirm Japan's status with our vet or the EU checker.

Mui's timeline

  1. 3+ months before

    Vet checks Mui's microchip is ISO 11784/11785 and that her rabies shot came after the chip and is still valid.

  2. 2–3 months before

    Book Mui on Iberia and buy a crate that fits their size rules. Airlines have limited pet spots.

  3. At least 2 weeks before

    Notify Japan's Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) and apply for the export inspection.

  4. Final 10 days

    Vet exam and EU animal health certificate. It must be signed by an official vet no more than 10 days before Mui arrives in the EU.

  5. Arrival

    Documents and microchip are checked at the airport of entry. No quarantine if everything is in order.

  6. In Valencia

    Register Mui with the city, find a vet, and get her EU pet passport for future trips.

If Mui might ever go back to Japan: Japan's re-entry rules need two rabies shots, an antibody test at an approved lab and a long waiting period. Ask AQS before we leave whether doing the antibody test in Japan now would shorten that later.

In Spain

  • Choose a flat that accepts dogs in writing (the Valencia homes page only lists pet-friendly ones).
  • Register Mui with the Valencia city council and a local vet.
  • Check the current rules on dog-owner liability insurance under Spain's animal welfare law.
  • Get the EU pet passport so Mui can travel within Europe.
